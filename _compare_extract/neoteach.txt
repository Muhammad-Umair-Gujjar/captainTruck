Abstract
The rapid evolution of digital education has created a growing demand for intelligent,
personalized, and automated learning platforms that go beyond the limitations of traditional
e-learning systems. NeoTeach is an AI-powered course builder and personalized learning
platform developed to address these gaps by integrating artificial intelligence,
gamification, and real-time analytics into a unified educational ecosystem. Built using
Flutter and Dart for cross-platform mobile deployment, Node.js for backend processing,
and MongoDB for scalable data storage, NeoTeach enables teachers to instantly generate
structured courses, lessons, quizzes, and assignments through natural language prompts
processed by the OpenAI or Gemini API, drastically reducing content preparation time and
allowing instructors to focus on meaningful student interaction. For students, the platform
delivers a highly engaging and personalized learning experience through AI-driven quiz
generation with instant auto-grading, progress tracking dashboards, and a gamification
engine that awards badges, maintains daily streaks, and displays competitive leaderboards
to sustain motivation. The system further incorporates a tutoring marketplace where
teachers can publish sessions and students can book one-on-one or group tutoring slots, a
real-time communication hub for student-teacher interaction, and a secure payment module
supporting Stripe, JazzCash, and Easypaisa. Administrative functionality includes
comprehensive user management, course moderation, content removal, activity
monitoring, and platform-wide reporting to ensure quality control and system integrity.
NeoTeach targets students, freelance educators, and academic institutions seeking an
affordable, scalable, and AI-driven alternative to conventional learning management
systems, positioning itself as a complete solution that simultaneously reduces instructor
workload, enhances student engagement, and delivers measurable learning outcomes
through data-driven insights.

1.4.2. Project Cost Estimation by using COCOMO’81 (Constructive Cost Model)
Boehm's COCOMO model is one of the mostly used models commercially. The first
version of the model was delivered in 1981 and COCOMO II is available now. COCOMO
81 is a model that allows one to estimate the cost, effort, and schedule when planning a
new software development activity.
Three levels:
Basic: Is used mostly for rough, early estimates.
Intermediate: Is the most used version including 15 different factors to account
for the influence of various project attributes such as personnel capability, use of
modern tools.
Detailed: Accounts for the influence of the different factors on individual project
phases: design, coding/testing, and integration/testing.
Each level includes three software development types:
1. Organic: Relatively small software teams develop familiar types of software in an
in-house environment.
2. Embedded: The project may require new technology, unfamiliar algorithms, or an
innovative new method
3. Semi-detached: Is an intermediate stage between organic and embedded types.

1.6. CPM - Critical Path Method
In 1957, DuPont developed a project management method designed to address the
challenge of shutting down chemical plants for maintenance and then restarting the plants
once the maintenance had been completed.
CPM provides the following benefits:
• Provides a graphical view of the project.
• Predicts the time required to complete the project.
CPM models the activities and events of a project as a network. Activities are depicted as
nodes on the network and events that signify the beginning or ending of activities are
depicted as arcs or lines between the nodes. The following is an example of a CPM network
diagram:
Steps in CPM Project Planning
1. Specify the individual activities.
2. Determine the sequence of those activities.
3. Draw a network diagram.
4. Estimate the completion time for each activity.
5. Identify the critical path (longest path through the network)
6. Update the CPM diagram as the project progresses.
