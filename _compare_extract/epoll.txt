Chapter 1: Project Feasibility Report
1.1 Introduction
Organizational elections — university societies, companies, clubs and similar bodies — are
still often run with paper ballots or ad-hoc digital tools that are hard to trust. Paper processes
are slow and error-prone. Centralized e-voting systems store ballots in a single database, which
makes independent audit of vote integrity difficult. Commercial SaaS election products solve
usability for many organizations but typically remain centralized. Academic Hyperledger
Fabric samples prove that permissioned ledgers can record votes, yet they are rarely packaged
as a full multi-tenant SaaS with web administration and a dedicated mobile voting app.
E-Poll is a multi-tenant Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) election management platform.
Organizations register on a public website, manage elections through web admin consoles, and
voters cast ballots through an Android Flutter application. The NestJS backend enforces
authentication, tenancy and election rules in PostgreSQL, and submits each successful vote to
a Hyperledger Fabric permissioned network (approximately ten nodes for demonstration) so
that vote integrity is backed by an immutable ledger transaction ID.
This chapter assesses whether the project is feasible, fixes its scope, estimates its cost and
schedule, and introduces the team, tools, vision, risks and product features. Development
follows the Incremental Model: after a planning phase, the system is delivered in four
increments, each designed, built and tested before the next is added.
1.2 Project/Product Feasibility Report
Feasibility measures how practical the system is to build and use with the staff, skills,
equipment and time available. E-Poll was assessed in eight areas: technical, operational,
economic, schedule, specification, information, motivational, and legal and ethical.

1.2.2 Operational Feasibility
Organization administrators need to create elections, manage candidates and voters, and control
lifecycle (schedule → open → close → publish) without developer help; E-Poll places these
flows in Org Admin and Super Admin web panels with the shared Ledger Ink shell. Voters
need a simple ballot and clear confirmation with a Fabric transaction reference in the Flutter
app. Platform operators need cross-tenant oversight without touching day-to-day org setup.
Compared with brand-locked or single-election Fabric demos, E-Poll targets multi-
organization self-serve elections with an integrated mobile vote path.
Verdict: Operationally feasible.
1.2.3 Economic Feasibility
Function Point Analysis (Section 1.4.1) values the development effort at 24.74 person-
months, or PKR 2,226,420 at market rates. For an FYP this labour is provided by the team, so
actual spending is limited to developer tooling, electricity/internet, optional cloud frontend
hosting, printing and documentation. On the benefit side, organizations gain reusable election
infrastructure; voters gain transparent confirmations; the university gains a demonstrable
Fabric + SaaS teaching artifact. Intangible benefits include trust, auditability and team industry
skills.

1.2.8 Legal & Ethical Feasibility
• Personal data (emails, names, org profiles) will be handled in line with Pakistan’s
Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016 principles: minimize collection,
protect credentials (argon2 hashes), and restrict access by role.
• Votes on the ledger are designed for integrity and audit, not public disclosure of
individual choices in the admin audit view.
• JWT sessions and role checks prevent cross-tenant data access; suspended
organizations are blocked at the API.
• Licences: NestJS, Next.js, Flutter, Prisma, Hyperledger Fabric and supporting packages
are open source (MIT/Apache/BSD-style); no proprietary election-engine licence is
required for the FYP.
• The system does not claim legal certification for government national elections.
Verdict: No infringement or liability is expected for the academic scope.

Size was derived from the function points at 40 LOC per FP, which is lower than the 53
LOC/FP common for object-oriented languages because Nest modules, Prisma, Next.js and
Flutter widgets replace much of the hand-written code. Early Design fits E-Poll: the
requirements and architecture are fixed in the planning phase (task B), Nest API contracts and
Prisma models are settled, and construction of remaining increments continues under that
baseline. The model is:
PM = A × Size^E × Π EMi , where E = B + 0.01 × Σ SFj
TDEV = C × PM^(D + 0.2 × 0.01 × Σ SFj)
PM is effort in person-months and Size is new code in KLOC. A = 2.94 and B = 0.91 are
calibration constants, SFj are the five scale factors (PREC, FLEX, RESL, TEAM, PMAT) and
EMi the seven Early Design effort multipliers (RCPX, RUSE, PDIF, PERS, PREX, FCIL,
SCED). TDEV is the schedule in months, with C = 3.67 and D = 0.28. These equations and the
nominal values below are the published COCOMO II.2000 calibration (Boehm et al., Software
Cost Estimation with COCOMO II, Prentice Hall, 2000). Lecture 6 names the Early Design
model but does not give its equations, so they are standard formulas still to be confirmed with
the course instructor.
With all scale factors and effort multipliers at Nominal (Σ SF = 18.96, Π EM = 1.00):
KLOC = 247.38 × 40 / 1000 = 9.90
E = 0.91 + 0.01 × 18.96 = 1.0996
PM = 2.94 × (9.90)^1.0996 = 36.57 person-months
TDEV = 3.67 × (36.57)^0.3179 = 11.52 months
People = PM / TDEV = 36.57 / 11.52 = 3.17 ≈ 3
COCOMO II also points to a team of about 3, but at nominal ratings it needs 36.57 person-
months over 11.52 months: more effort than the function point estimate (24.74) and longer than
our 26-week critical path. The gap comes mainly from the nominal assumption. NestJS,
Next.js, Flutter and the already-frozen backend may justify better-than-nominal ratings for
tools (FCIL) and experience (PREX, PERS), which would lower the effort, but we will apply
them only after agreeing the ratings with our supervisor. The size of 9.90 KLOC also rests on
the assumed 40 LOC/FP.
Result: COCOMO II Early Design (nominal): 9.90 KLOC, 36.57 person-months, PKR
3,291,300, 11.52 months, about 3 people.

For each activity we computed the earliest start and finish (ES, EF), latest start and finish (LS,
LF), total slack (TS = LS − ES) and free slack (FS = earliest ES of successors − EF). Activities
with zero slack form the critical path.
