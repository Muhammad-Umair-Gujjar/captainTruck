1.1 Introduction
Truck-based logistics depends on coordination among customers, transport agents, truck owners, drivers, and administrators.
Booking and dispatch activities may be managed through calls, messages, paper records, and separate tracking tools. These
fragmented workflows can make it difficult to compare routes, establish an agreed fare, confirm driver assignment, monitor a
trip, and reconcile collected money.
Captain Truck is a mobile-and-web logistics and dispatch platform designed to coordinate full-truck transportation requests
from initial booking to trip completion and financial reconciliation. It will provide three connected interfaces: a Flutter Customer
Mobile Application, a Flutter Driver Mobile Application, and a React-based Web Admin Dashboard. A NestJS backend will
enforce business rules and connect the clients to a PostgreSQL database and external services.
The platform will combine OpenStreetMap-based geocoding and routing, an on-device AI-assisted fare estimate,
customer-proposed pricing within an administrator-configured adjustment range, human-reviewed dispatch and driver
assignment, real-time GPS tracking, offline map access for drivers, in-app chat, notifications, media uploads, and two
payment collection paths. Cash payments will be collected by the driver; online payments will be collected by the platform
through Stripe Test Mode for demonstration and testing.
Captain Truck is intended as a digital full-truckload booking and dispatch platform inspired by the intermediary role found in
Pakistan’s transport market. It is not proposed as a complete consignment/bilty management system; detailed multi-sender
load consolidation and full goods-consignment accounting are outside the current scope.
1.1.1 Problem Statement
Customers may find it difficult to compare route options and understand an estimated freight amount before requesting a
truck. Drivers and operators may rely on manual communication to receive trip details and report progress. Administrators
may need to coordinate approvals, driver willingness, assignments, trip monitoring, and commission collection across
separate channels. Cash and online transactions also require distinct records to prevent confusion between customer fare,
platform commission, and amounts owed to a truck operator.
The project addresses these problems by bringing dispatch requests, route comparison, AI-assisted pricing, admin approval,
driver assignment, live location, trip communication, payment records, and reconciliation into one controlled platform.
1.1.2 Proposed Solution and Distinguishing Features
• Customer-adjustable AI estimate: the model suggests a fare, but the customer proposes the final amount within a
configurable range, such as ±10% or ±20%. The admin can approve it or reject it with a reason.
• Admin-mediated assignment: the system does not automatically assign a driver. The admin sends a direct willingness
inquiry to a suitable driver and assigns the trip after confirmation.
• Route comparison with price context: the customer can compare up to three OSRM route alternatives using distance,
estimated duration, route geometry, and an estimated cost.
• Offline-aware driver map: the driver downloads supported map tiles for the planned route area before departure. The app
can display cached maps and route details when internet connectivity is lost.
• Hybrid payment and reconciliation: cash is collected by the driver; online payments are collected by the platform. The
system maintains separate records for fare payment, platform commission, driver collection, operator payable, and
remittance.
• On-device assistance: the prototype uses a TensorFlow Lite-compatible fare-estimation model and ML Kit Smart Reply
suggestions, avoiding a paid cloud generative-AI dependency.
1.1.3 Comparative Positioning
Captain Truck belongs to the digital truck-booking and freight-dispatch category. Freight marketplaces, load boards, and
traditional transport agents already address parts of the same problem. Captain Truck’s proposed emphasis is a single
controlled workflow combining customer route selection, an AI-informed but customer-adjustable fare, admin-mediated
assignment, offline-aware driver mapping, live tracking, chat, and method-specific financial reconciliation.

Captain Truck: A Comprehensive Real-Time Logistics and Dispatch Platform
1.3 Project Overview Statement
Captain Truck provides a coordinated ecosystem for customers who need full-truck transportation, drivers who fulfil approved
trips, and administrators who manage dispatch operations. Customers submit pickup and destination details, select a route,
review an AI-suggested estimate, propose a fare, and agree to pay the amount they submit. Administrators review the
request, approve or reject it, and arrange a willing driver and truck. During an active trip, authorized users can view location
updates, communicate through in-app chat, and receive notifications. The system records cash and online payment flows
separately and supports commission reconciliation through an auditable ledger.

1.4 Project Goal and Objectives
Project Goal: To develop a secure, real-time logistics and dispatch platform that connects customers with truck drivers,
supports route selection and AI-assisted customer-proposed pricing, enables admin-controlled dispatch and trip monitoring,
and records cash and online payments with transparent financial reconciliation.
• Develop secure authentication, JWT access/refresh token handling, logout, account status controls, and role-based
authorization for customers, drivers, and administrators.
• Build customer and driver Flutter applications and a React/Vite/Tailwind admin dashboard connected to a NestJS API and
PostgreSQL database.
• Enable customers to submit truck requests, compare up to three route options, receive an AI estimate, adjust the proposed
fare within an admin-configured limit, and track request status.
• Implement admin approval/rejection with a mandatory reason, direct driver-willingness inquiry, and manual driver/truck
assignment.
• Provide trip lifecycle management, real-time GPS tracking, offline map access for drivers, in-app chat, media upload, and
push notifications.
• Implement on-device fare estimation and Smart Reply as bounded FYP prototype features, with documented evaluation
and limitations.
• Support cash collection by the driver and online payment collection by the platform through Stripe Test Mode, with distinct
reconciliation paths and verified server-side payment status.
• Evaluate security, functional correctness, usability, offline recovery, route selection, payment-state transitions, and
financial-ledger accuracy using defined test cases and representative users.
1.4.1 Project Success Criteria
• A user can register or log in and only access functionality permitted for their role.
• A customer can create a dispatch request, compare available routes, submit a fare proposal within the allowed range, and
see the request status.
• An administrator can review a request, approve or reject it with a reason, ask a driver whether they are willing to accept it,
and assign a confirmed driver and truck.
• A driver can access assigned trip details, update trip status, use cached map data where available, and share location
updates when connectivity permits.
• Customer and administrator tracking views display the latest server-received location and its timestamp; stale/offline status
is shown when updates stop.

1.4.2 Assumptions, Risks and Obstacles
Assumptions: customers and drivers have compatible smartphones; drivers grant GPS permissions; the driver downloads
supported map data before travel when offline access is required; administrators register and manage driver accounts; and
external mapping, notification, media, and payment test services remain available under their terms.
Risks: public geocoding/routing services may rate-limit or become unavailable; offline tile coverage and storage vary by
device and provider; GPS can be inaccurate or delayed; AI training data may be sparse; cash collection and remittance may
be disputed; online payment webhooks may be delayed or repeated; and payment-provider production eligibility may differ
from test-mode availability.
Mitigations: use timeouts and graceful fallbacks for external services; cache route details and supported map tiles; show
location freshness; validate fares on the backend; use unique payment references and idempotent webhook processing;
maintain audit records; and clearly label Stripe integration as test-only for this FYP unless production eligibility is
independently confirmed.
1.5 High-Level System Components
The platform has three client applications, a central API/business-logic tier, a persistent data tier, a real-time communication
layer, external service integrations, and on-device capabilities. The 15 functional modules are described in Section 1.6.
1.6 Functional Modules and How They Work
Module 1 — Authentication and Authorization
Customers can register and log in. Drivers are created by an administrator rather than through public driver registration. The
system issues short-lived JWT access tokens and refresh tokens, supports logout and a current-user endpoint, and applies
role-based access control for Customer, Driver, and Admin. Repeated failed logins trigger a temporary lockout or rate limit.
Passwords are stored using a suitable password-hashing algorithm; tokens and secrets are not stored in plaintext.
Module 2 — User Management
Users can view and update permitted profile fields. Driver profiles include licence details and availability information.
Administrators can search and filter users and drivers, enable or disable accounts, edit administrative profile fields, and
create driver accounts. Disabled accounts cannot start new authenticated sessions or perform protected actions.
Module 3 — Truck Types and Base Rates
Administrators manage truck categories and their base rates. Rate changes are versioned with an effective date. When a
dispatch request is created, the applicable rate version is copied into the request so later rate changes do not silently alter an
existing proposal. Example truck categories and rates are configurable demonstration data, not fixed market prices.
Module 4 — Dispatch Request Management
A customer enters truck count, truck type(s), pickup and drop-off addresses, optional map pins, desired pickup date, and
notes. The request follows a controlled lifecycle such as Draft (if drafts are implemented), Awaiting Route Selection, Pending
Admin, Approved or Rejected, and then Assigned once a driver/truck is confirmed. Cancellation is permitted only in defined
pre-trip states. A trip record is created only after the request has been approved and assigned.

Module 5 — Route Selection and OpenStreetMap Integration
The app uses geocoding/autocomplete services such as Nominatim and Photon and requests route alternatives from OSRM.
When available, it displays up to three alternatives with distance, estimated duration, and route geometry. The fastest route
can be labelled Recommended, but the customer remains able to select another available route. If only one route is returned
or routing fails, the app displays the available result or a clear retry/fallback message. Public service usage must follow the
respective providers’ policies.
Module 6 — AI-Assisted Fare Estimation and Customer Proposal
A TensorFlow Lite-compatible on-device model provides an estimated trip cost using the prototype’s available features,
initially distance-based. The estimate is combined with the locked truck-type base rate and truck count according to the
documented pricing formula. The customer may propose an amount within an administrator-configured adjustment band
around the estimate (for example ±10% or ±20%). The backend validates the range and records the estimate, adjustment
limit, customer proposal, and rate version. The submitted proposal is not treated as approved until the administrator accepts
it. The AI result is advisory, not a guarantee of market price.
The prototype may use the most recent 20 completed trips as a limited training/evaluation window. Training/retraining and
model-version distribution must be implemented as a separate supported process; TensorFlow Lite inference alone does not
automatically train a model. The final implementation will document exactly where training occurs and how model versions
are evaluated.
Module 7 — Admin Approval, Driver Willingness and Assignment
Administrators review route details, truck count/type, the AI estimate, the customer’s proposed fare, the allowed adjustment
band, and the total bill. They may approve or reject the request; rejection requires a reason visible to the customer. For
approved requests, the admin directly asks a candidate driver whether they are willing to accept the trip. Only after a positive
response does the admin assign the driver and truck. There is no automatic driver matching. The approved fare is locked;
changes require an explicit amendment or reapproval record.
Module 8 — Trip Management
A trip becomes active only after request approval and assignment. The driver receives pickup, destination, route, customer
contact/chat, and payment instructions. The driver updates defined statuses such as Assigned, En Route to Pickup, Arrived
at Pickup, In Transit, Arrived at Destination, and Completed. The system validates allowed transitions and records
timestamps. Payment settlement and trip completion are related but separate states; exceptional completion with an
outstanding payment must follow an explicit admin-controlled rule.
Module 9 — Real-Time GPS Tracking and Offline Maps
The driver app sends GPS updates during the permitted trip stages through Socket.IO. The backend associates updates with
the correct trip and broadcasts them to authorized customer and admin views. The system stores the latest received location
and timestamp and handles temporary disconnection by showing stale status. The driver downloads supported map tiles for
the planned route area before departure and caches route geometry and essential trip details. GPS can continue locally
without internet, but remote users cannot receive live updates until connectivity returns. Queued updates must be
timestamped so delayed points are not mistaken for the current location. Offline map downloads must use a tile provider
whose licence and terms permit the intended caching.
Module 10 — In-App Chat and Smart Reply
The platform supports trip-related customer-driver and driver-admin conversations. Messages can include text, images, voice
notes, and preset messages. Socket.IO provides live delivery where connected; message records remain in the database.
ML Kit Smart Reply may suggest up to three English replies based on recent messages on the device. Suggestions are
optional and are not sent automatically. Reporting, input validation, access checks, and a documented 90-day
message-retention/cleanup policy are included. Chat becomes read-only after the relevant trip is closed, subject to the final
operational rules.
Module 11 — Media Upload
The client requests a short-lived signed upload configuration from the backend and uploads supported images or voice files
directly to Cloudinary. The backend stores only validated metadata and the resulting asset reference. File type, size, access
permissions, and ownership are checked. Secret Cloudinary credentials remain on the server; they are never embedded in
the mobile or web clients.

Module 12 — Payments: Cash and Stripe Test Mode
Captain Truck uses two payment paths. For cash, the driver collects the agreed fare at pickup and records the collection; the
platform tracks the expected amount and any confirmation or discrepancy. For online payment, the customer pays through
Stripe Test Mode, the platform receives the payment, and the backend verifies the final state through signed webhooks and
provider references. A browser/app success redirect alone does not mark a payment as paid. Repeated webhook events are
handled idempotently. The Stripe integration is for demonstration and testing; production availability for the intended business
must be verified separately.
Payment states are distinct from trip states. The system records the agreed fare, payment method, amount,
transaction/reference ID, status, timestamps, and any failure or refund/test reversal where implemented. Online collection
creates an amount payable to the truck operator after the platform’s agreed commission and any explicitly modelled
adjustments. Cash collection creates a potential commission remittance due from the driver/operator to the platform. These
are separate accounting directions.
Module 13 — Commission Ledger and Reconciliation
For each trip, the backend calculates the platform commission according to the configured policy and records it separately
from the customer fare. For cash trips, the ledger records expected commission, amount remitted, outstanding balance, and
admin confirmation. For online trips, the ledger records the platform-collected fare, commission, and operator payable. The
admin can review discrepancies and record permitted adjustments with a reason and audit trail. Financial values are stored
using integer minor units (for example, paisa) rather than floating-point numbers.
Module 14 — Push Notifications
The client registers and refreshes its Firebase Cloud Messaging token with the backend and can remove it on logout or token
rotation. Notifications inform users about request submission, approval/rejection, driver willingness/assignment, trip status
changes, payment events, and other defined events. Notifications contain minimal sensitive information; the app retrieves
protected details from the API after authorization. FCM is used for push delivery, not as the primary database.
Module 15 — Admin Dashboard and Operational Controls
The React, Vite, and Tailwind dashboard gives administrators a central view of dispatch counts, pending requests, request
details, routes, fare estimates and proposals, approvals/rejections, driver willingness, assignments, active trips, user/driver
management, truck types and rate history, payment records, commission ledgers, and operational status. The dashboard
communicates with the NestJS API; it does not directly modify PostgreSQL or bypass backend authorization. Live trip
locations and operational updates use authorized real-time channels.
1.7 Exclusions and Boundaries
• No live Stripe merchant/payment production claim; Stripe Test Mode is used for FYP demonstration unless production
eligibility is independently confirmed.
• No in-app wallet, escrow, automatic bank payout, or automated settlement to truck operators.
• No automatic driver matching, bidding marketplace, or driver broadcast; admin-mediated willingness and assignment are
used.
• No hardware GPS trackers, vehicle telematics, IoT sensors, or fuel sensors; tracking relies on the driver’s smartphone.
• No NADRA integration, biometric verification, or automated government identity verification.
• No multi-stop route planning or manual route editing in the initial scope.
• No guaranteed production-grade AI pricing accuracy; the model is an evaluated FYP prototype and its output remains
advisory.
• No full traditional bilty/consignment workflow, multi-sender cargo consolidation, warehouse management, or freight
forwarding suite.
• No multilingual UI in the initial release; Smart Reply is English-only.
• Advanced analytics/export, rating and review, and other optional features may be deferred if the core modules require
additional time.

1.8 Application Architecture
1. Presentation Tier
Flutter Customer App and Flutter Driver App target Android and, where supported by testing resources, iOS. The
React.js/Vite/Tailwind Admin Dashboard runs in a modern browser.
2. API and Business Logic Tier
NestJS exposes versioned REST endpoints under /api/v1 for account management, truck types, dispatch requests, routes,
fare proposals, approvals, assignments, trips, payments, ledger entries, media signing, and notifications. DTO validation,
authentication guards, role checks, rate limits, and service-layer business rules are enforced on the backend.
3. Persistent Data Tier
PostgreSQL stores users, driver profiles, truck types, rate versions, dispatch requests, route options/selections, fare
proposals, trips, assignments, GPS latest/history records as scoped by retention policy, conversations, messages, media
metadata, payment records, commission entries, remittances, notifications, and audit events. Transactions and constraints
protect important state changes and financial consistency.
4. Real-Time Layer
Socket.IO supports authenticated trip-room location updates, chat delivery, and authorized admin operational events. Redis
may be added as a Socket.IO adapter/shared coordination layer when multiple backend instances are deployed; it is not the
primary database. A single-instance FYP deployment can use a simpler configuration while preserving authorization and
room boundaries.
5. External Services
OpenStreetMap-related services provide map tiles, geocoding/autocomplete, and OSRM routing; Cloudinary handles media
assets; Firebase Cloud Messaging delivers push notifications; Stripe Test Mode demonstrates online payment flow.
External-service failure must not bypass backend validation or corrupt financial state.
6. On-Device AI and Offline Capability
TFLite-compatible inference estimates trip cost locally; ML Kit provides optional Smart Reply suggestions. The driver app
caches route details and permitted map tiles for offline display. Local GPS collection can continue offline, but server-side
tracking, remote chat, notifications, and payment verification require connectivity and synchronize or resume when service
returns.
Dispatch and payment flow
Customer authenticates → creates request → selects route → receives AI estimate → submits an allowed fare proposal →
backend validates and records it → admin approves or rejects → admin confirms driver willingness and assigns driver/truck
→ trip begins → driver and customer/admin use tracking/chat → fare is collected through cash or test-mode online payment
→ platform commission and operator payable/remittance are reconciled → trip is closed under the defined status rules.

1.12 Security, Privacy, and Data Management
• Enforce role checks and object-level access on the backend; a user must not access another customer’s request or an
unrelated trip’s GPS/chat records.
• Use TLS in deployment, password hashing, secure token handling, environment-managed secrets, validation, rate limiting,
and safe error messages.
• Verify Stripe webhook signatures and process events idempotently. Never trust client-submitted payment status.
• Keep financial changes auditable. Correct mistakes through explicit adjustment entries rather than silently overwriting
ledger history.
• Limit GPS access to authorized active-trip participants and administrators with a legitimate operational role. Show location
freshness and define a retention period.
• Retain chat media and messages according to the proposed 90-day cleanup policy, subject to final privacy and audit
requirements. Define separate retention rules for financial and trip records.
• Use a map tile provider whose terms permit offline caching; do not assume public OpenStreetMap tile servers permit bulk
offline downloads.
• Avoid placing private customer addresses, tokens, or sensitive payment details in push-notification payloads or application
logs.
1.13 Testing and Evaluation
• Unit tests for fare calculations, adjustment limits, commission calculations, role checks, and trip-state transitions.
• API integration tests for request submission, approval/rejection, assignment, payment creation, webhook processing, and

• End-to-end tests covering customer, admin, and driver workflows, including rejection and resubmission of a fare proposal.
• Security tests for unauthorized access, disabled accounts, invalid tokens, cross-user data access, repeated webhook

• Offline tests for cached map display, local GPS collection, stale-location indicators, reconnection, and delayed update
ordering.
• AI evaluation comparing the model’s predictions with a simple baseline on available completed-trip data; report dataset
size, error metric, and limitations.
• Usability evaluation with representative users and a documented set of tasks; record task completion, errors, and
feedback.

1.15 Conclusion
Captain Truck proposes a unified, role-controlled platform for full-truck booking and dispatch. By combining route alternatives,
AI-assisted customer-proposed pricing, administrator-led assignment, live and offline-aware trip support, chat, notifications,
and method-specific financial reconciliation, the project provides substantial work across mobile development, web
development, backend engineering, databases, real-time systems, mapping, applied machine learning, security, and software
testing. The implementation will prioritize a consistent architecture and verifiable end-to-end workflows over unsupported
claims of fully automated logistics or production-grade AI.
